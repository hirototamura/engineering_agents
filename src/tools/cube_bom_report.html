We compared the reference cube’s bill of materials (BOM) with 22 human-reviewed requirements, then ran one frozen, assumption-based configuration through 34 simulations.

The supplied BOM establishes component identities. It contains no measured part masses and no calibrated motor curve. These results describe a numerical reference, not a calibrated replica or a hardware acceptance test. No LLM calls or design iterations were performed.

04 · From components to numerical inputs
A functional reference with explicit physical priors


What is actually simulated
A free rigid body, three spinning wheel bodies and eight approximate spherical corner contacts on a floor. The three wheel assemblies sit on the faces that meet at corner 000. Gravity, contact, inertia, motor clipping, sensor noise and delay are active.
The saved LQR (linear-quadratic regulator) gain matrix is retained without tuning. Physics runs at 1 ms; control at 15 ms. The source project’s firmware is inspected but is not executed or translated to SI torque.
What the part identities do not yet predict
No battery voltage sag, bus current, electrical energy, temperature, printed-part flexibility, fastener strength or calibrated PWM response. No raw MPU6050 accelerometer/filter model or encoder quantization. No seven-other-corner evaluation.
The source firmware declares a 15 ms loop, 20 kHz 8-bit PWM, a complementary-filter weight of 0.996 and gyro-filter coefficient 0.7. These firmware facts are recorded separately from the simulation model.




Mass accounting: 0.550 kg fixed aggregate + 3 × 0.060 kg rotating aggregate = 0.730 kg. The 0.049 kg candidate battery is included in the fixed aggregate, leaving an unresolved 0.501 kg fixed-mass budget. Motor stators are in the frame; motor rotors are in the wheel aggregates. These inherited budgets are not measured BOM totals.

05 · Fixed simulations
Nominal balance succeeds; the broader test matrix exposes weaknesses




24 short development cases
Each six-second partition contains three coupled domains × two release/pulse conditions × two noise seeds. “Exploration” and “validation” are retained file labels; both matrices are disclosed development tests. They are not fresh hardware validation.
Nominal: mass ×1, friction 0.7, corner radius 4 mm, delay 15 ms. Heavy/delayed: mass ×1.15 (×1.173 in the second partition), friction 0.4, radius 8 mm, delay 30 ms. Light: mass ×0.85, friction 0.9, radius 2 mm, delay 15 ms.
Ten separate 30-second diagnostics
One nominal geometry and release, no external torque pulse, with sensor-noise seeds 2101–2110. We check duration, peak tilt, whole-interval RMS, drift proxy and contact. This tests the model under fixed conditions; it does not establish a 9/10 hardware success rate or eight-corner operation.




Saved body poses and wheel angles; simplified geometry, not CAD. Rotation is shown at sampled times and can alias fast wheel motion. Cyan floor point marks the initial support.

06 · What the results mean
Separate balance, actuator workload and missing evidence


Score = passing fraction − 0.05 × mean final-second tilt RMS [deg] − 0.2 × mean torque-threshold fraction




Twenty of 24 short cases exceed the torque comparison gate. In fourteen of these cases the model completes six seconds without losing balance. A failed gate is therefore not the same as a fall. The 0.04 N m comparison value is uncalibrated; exceeding it does not prove that a real Nidec motor overheats. Six heavy/delayed cases do lose balance, so their failures are not merely a scoring-label issue.


The heavy domain changes mass, contact geometry, friction and delay together. These runs do not isolate which change causes the failure. The 30-second nominal results and the short stressed results answer different questions; averaging them into one success rate would hide that distinction.


Useful conclusions now
The assumed architecture can remain upright for 30 seconds in nominal, undisturbed simulations with the frozen controller. The broader, coupled domain matrix is not robust. Component mapping exposes what has to be measured before these findings can guide hardware decisions.
Next measurements, before optimization
  1. Resolve wheel/holder variants; obtain exact STL dimensions and as-built masses.
  2. Measure wheel inertia and the full cube’s center of mass.
  3. Calibrate torque/speed/PWM/braking at the actual battery voltage.
  4. Measure sensing delay/noise and floor/contact behaviour.
  5. Add independent metrology and synchronized current/temperature logs.


No requirements, score coefficients, stop limits or historical results were changed. No new design was selected as an adopted baseline.
